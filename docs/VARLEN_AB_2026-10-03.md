# Variable-length A/B: does a bigger search space find an edge?
Date: 2026-10-03. Commit 259656c (harness), 0425733 (variable length + unbiased seeds).

## What was run
Four arms, 8 seeds each, 32 walkforward runs. Grammar reduced, reward v3, objective v3,
selection keyed on train only, real Binance funding.
| arm | genome length | data |
|---|---|---|| fixed-12 | exactly 12 tokens | real |
| fixed-12 | exactly 12 tokens | iid null |
| varlen | drawn from 6..20 | real |
| varlen | drawn from 6..20 | iid null |
10 generations, population 16, 5 folds, seeds 101-108. Command:
```.venv2/bin/python research/run_varlen_ab_28c.py --seeds 8 --generations 10 \    --population 16 --folds 5 --workers 8 --outdir results/varlen_ab.venv2/bin/python research/analyze_varlen_ab_28c.py --outdir results/varlen_ab --json```
This is a DIAGNOSTIC, not a reportable result. It is smaller than the recorded pilot
(25 generations, 10 folds) and no gate was applied.
## Why the null arm carries the decision
A bigger search space finds a better fit to whatever is there. If what is there is
noise, a bigger search space fits the noise better and the real-data score goes UP
for the wrong reason. So the real-data column is not evidence on its own. The
comparison that carries information is the real-minus-null gap computed INSIDE
each arm, because the null is the same search pointed at data with no edge in it.
Sanity checks passed: buy-and-hold Sharpe is +1.418 in all 16 real runs (identical
bars, as it must be) and differs per seed in all 16 null runs (different
synthetic bars, as it must be).
## Result 1: neither arm beats buy-and-hold

Holdout excess Sharpe over buy-and-hold, which is the quantity the acceptance gate
tests. Mean +/- standard error over 8 seeds.

| arm | data | excess Sharpe | seeds beating the hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed-12 | real | -3.192 +/- 0.880 | 1 / 8 |
| fixed-12 | null | -1.333 +/- 0.398 | 2 / 8 |
| varlen | real | -1.767 +/- 0.564 | 2 / 8 |
| varlen | null | -2.604 +/- 0.803 | 0 / 8 |

**3 of 16 real runs beat buy-and-hold.** Absolute Sharpe is positive in most runs
(fixed-12 real +1.32) but buy-and-hold on the same bars is +1.42, so the book is
tracking the drift and losing to simply holding. Six of the eight fixed-12 real runs
and six of the eight varlen real runs sit at the 0.25 position cap, which means the
search is mostly finding "be long", not a timing rule.

## Result 2: the real-null gap is not distinguishable from zero

| arm | real | null | gap | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed-12 | -3.192 | -1.333 | **-1.860** | -1.93 |
| varlen | -1.767 | -2.604 | **+0.838** | +0.85 |

Fixed-12 is significantly NEGATIVE: real data does WORSE than synthetic data. That is
not a weak edge, it is an actively harmful one, and it is the signature of selection
fitting the training window.

Varlen is positive but +0.84 +/- 0.98 is entirely consistent with zero.

Difference of the two gaps: **+2.70 +/- 1.38, t = +1.96** on 8 seeds per cell. That is
the boundary of significance and it is NOT a result.

## Result 3: the gap widening is the null arm getting worse, not real getting better

| data | varlen | fixed-12 | gain from varlen |
|---|---|---|---|
| real | -1.767 | -3.192 | **+1.426** |
| null | -2.604 | -1.333 | **-1.272** |

The variable arm scores better on real data. It also scores 1.27 WORSE on null data,
which is a bigger move. The whole +2.70 gap-widening is the null arm degrading, not the
real arm improving. Reporting only the +1.426 real-data gain would read as a win.

## Result 4: the variable arm is not converging

| arm (real) | factors used | token count |
|---|---|---|
| fixed-12 | 5.75 (sd 0.46) | 12.00 (sd 0.00) |
| varlen | 5.50 (sd 2.67) | 12.12 (sd 4.82) |

Same mean factor count, six times the spread. Twelve independent searches return
twelve genomes with factor counts from 2 to 10. That is the same non-convergence the
reduced grammar was introduced to fix, returning now that the length is free. A search
with no stable answer across seeds is sampling, not finding.

## Conclusion

**The variable-length expansion did not find an edge.** Item 2 raised reachable factor
subsets from 61.3% to 99.7% of the 2^12 space and that is a real change to the
hypothesis class. It did not produce a real-data result that separates from the null,
and at this n the one apparent improvement sits at t = +1.96 with the wrong sign on
its two halves.

The more informative observation is Result 3 and it is not about length: giving the
search more room made it WORSE on synthetic data. That is overfitting, and it is the
thing to attack next, not the search space.
