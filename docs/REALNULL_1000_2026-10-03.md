# 1000 iterations: the null arm calibrates every beat-rate this project has reported

Date: 2026-10-03. 500 real + 500 iid-null walkforward runs, seeds 501-1000.

1h bars, 41 factors (36 + 5 multi-timeframe), reduced grammar, reward v3, objective
v3, train-only selection, 10 generations, population 16, 5 folds, real Binance
funding, `--scale-windows` so factor horizons stay at their 30m values.

Reproduce: `tools/analyze_realnull_gap_28c.py --dir results/f1h41_iter1000 --features 41`

## Result 1: the gap is significantly negative and it is large

| data | holdout excess Sharpe | sd | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| real | -2.7429 +/- 0.0291 | 0.651 | -2.816 |
| null | -1.1003 +/- 0.0832 | 1.860 | -0.992 |

Real minus null: **-1.6426 +/- 0.0881, t = -18.64**, 95% CI [-1.815, -1.470].

The median real run sits at the **p16** of the observed null distribution. Real data
performs like the worst sixth of runs on data with no edge in it by construction.

## Result 2: on data with no edge, the search beats buy-and-hold 27.6% of the time

| arm | beat the hold |
|---|---|
| null (n=500) | **138 / 500 = 27.6%** |
| real (n=500) | **2 / 500 = 0.4%** |

This is the number every previous "k of n beat the hold" should have been reported
beside and was not. A genetic search over these formulas, pointed at synthetic data
with no structure in it, produces a book that beats a constant long roughly one run
in four. **Beating buy-and-hold is not evidence of anything.**

And the real arm is not merely below that calibration, it is 70x below it. The
ordering is inverted: more structure in the data made the search worse, not better.

## Result 3: breadth separates harder than anything else measured here

| arm | positive coins | mean position | at the cap | absolute Sharpe | buy-and-hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real | **21.04 / 28** | 0.0243 | 7 / 500 | +1.7449 | +3.1062 |
| null | 13.96 / 28 | 0.0926 | 69 / 500 | +0.5021 | +1.1354 |

Breadth: **+7.08 coins, t = +24.47**. This is the strongest statistical signal in the
project and it is unambiguous: real data produces books that are positive on 21 of
28 coins against the null's 14.

It does not convert into excess. A book positive on 21 legs, with a mean position of
0.024 and an absolute Sharpe of +1.74 against a hold of +3.11, is a book that tracks a
rising universe and pays costs to do it. Breadth without excess is drift.

## Result 4: the factor set is fully used

All 41 factors appear across the 500 real runs and all 5 multi-timeframe factors are
used. Most-used is `4h_PARKINSON` at 230, ahead of every 1h factor; then HL_RANGE (163),
WEEKEND (123), RANGE_MEAN_20 (100), DELTA_RSI (100), `4h_EFFICIENCY` (100).

The search prefers the coarsest grid's volatility estimate, which is the
multi-timeframe thesis working as designed. A factor the search leans on hardest is a
factor the search can overfit hardest.

## Reading

Three facts together, and they are not the shape of "no edge":

1. Real data performs significantly WORSE than synthetic (t = -18.64).
2. Real data produces significantly BROADER books (+7.08 coins, t = +24.47).
3. Real data's beat-the-hold rate is 70x below the no-edge calibration.

A factor set with nothing in it would produce all three of the same sign as the null.
Instead the real arm responds to real structure, and the response is breadth, and the
breadth does not survive contact with the hold.

That is the signature of a selection mechanism that cannot tell a real effect from a
lucky fit, not of an absence of effect. Selection is argmax on a single training
window; a genome that fits one window's noise is indistinguishable from a genome that
fits a real pattern, and argmax prefers whichever fits the noise more tightly because
there is more noise to fit.

The next experiment this points at is the selection rule, not the feature set and not
the search. A defensible version holds the factors and the GA fixed and replaces
single-window argmax with selection on a multi-window average or a cross-validated
penalty, then asks whether the negative gap moves toward zero. If it does, the factors
had something in them. If it does not, they did not.
